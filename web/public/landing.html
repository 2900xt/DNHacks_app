The problem

You can look up who sells a drug in seconds.

You cannot look up who makes the ingredient inside it. Drug master file contents are confidential by design — so the single most important fact about a medicine's supply chain is the one nobody can query.

How it works

Three hops, all on public record.

01

Trace down

From a drug product to its active ingredient, and from there to the precursor underneath it. Layer-3 edges carry a citation or the loader refuses them.

02

Fan back out

Kill any node and see everything structurally downstream of it — the drugs, the products, the facilities, the countries.

03

Check the alternates

Who else holds an active filing and a US registration, ranked by whether moving there actually diversifies you.

Worked example — 6-APA

That is not a prediction that they will go short. It is exposure — disrupt the precursor and all six move at once.

What we got wrong

We tested our own headline assumption. It failed.

We assumed supplier concentration predicts shortage — the intuition this whole field runs on. Across 3,902 generics and 161 shortage events, it doesn't just fail to predict. It points the wrong way.

Refuted
Concentration (HHI)AUC 0.196
Few labelersAUC 0.191

Permutation test, 300 shuffles, p = 0.003. Holds in every stratum and at every cutoff we tried.

What does predict
Market sizeAUC 0.779
Sterile injectableAUC 0.660
Prior-year price erosionAUC 0.612

Combined, cross-validated AUC 0.70.

We kept the map and threw out the score.

The graph is still exactly true — real filings, real facilities, real signals. Six drugs sharing one precursor is a fact about structure, not a forecast. What broke was the prediction layer, so we removed it rather than ship it.
